Sizing verdict

4 GB is too tight. 8 GB / 4 vCPU / 80 GB SSD is the honest target. Disk is comfortable — the base image is node:22-alpine shared across all 36 services, and each app adds only 4.6 MB of dependencies on top of it.

Tell whoever you hire this, first

.env.example ships the service-token allowlist with every value empty. serviceAuth throws on an entry like chopz: rather than silently dropping that caller — so following the old quickstart produced every guarded service crash-looping at startup, not a degraded system. The generator step is now documented, and it is not optional: 

# after cp .env.example .env, before compose up
node scripts/generate-service-tokens.mjs >> .env

On the SFU

Self-hosting means use_external_ip, UDP forwarding, and TURN for restrictive networks — that is a WebRTC specialist at $300–800, not a generalist. Use LiveKit Cloud instead. About an hour of configuration, you pay usage, and you find out whether the product actually needs real audio before you pay somebody to run infrastructure for it.

Do not pay for a certificate

Let's Encrypt is free and auto-renewing. Anyone quoting you for an SSL certificate as a line item is quoting for something the tooling already does for nothing.

The line item nobody quotes

ANTHROPIC_API_KEY usage. V4 proxies to the model, and that is metered per token. If agents actually get used this will be your largest bill — larger than the server, by a wide margin. Set a spend cap before you launch, not after.

Blocker #4, in depthThe Postgres job, and who can do it

This is the one that can quietly break your ledger, so it gets its own section. The first thing to understand is that it is not the job it sounds like.

It is not a database migration

There is no SQL in this repo to translate. lib/persistence.js is a recursive Proxy over a plain in-memory object tree — routes do store.accounts[id].vcoin -= amount and the Proxy catches the mutation and flushes to disk. A contractor billing themselves as a Postgres migration expert will arrive expecting tables and find none.

The shape is far better than the headline suggests:

md5      lines  file

b573d8ba   109   vaco-shell         ← ESM port, same design

e09178c7    28   vdp     ┘ browser localStorage — NOT in scope

One design, three source files. Not thirty investigations. And commit(store) already exists as the durability seam, with a FLUSHERS WeakMap, so the swap point is a single function. Whoever takes this is replacing a storage backend behind a seam that has already been cut for them.

Two routes — insist on the first

RouteWhat changesEffort
Postgres JSONB Object tree stays. One row per store, a row lock around each mutation. Business logic essentially untouched. 80–150 h
Full normalization Real tables per app; every store.x.y.z access rewritten as a query. 3–5× that

If a candidate proposes full normalization out of the gate, that is a signal about them, not about the codebase. Decline it.

Who to go to

Not Fiverr, at any price. Not a DBA or query-tuning specialist either — there are no queries here to tune. What you want is a senior or staff Node.js backend engineer with real transactional-correctness experience: someone who has swapped a datastore before and talks fluently about isolation levels and read-modify-write windows.

  • Toptal, Gun.io, Braintrust, Arc — vetted, roughly $100–200/hr. You get a real senior and somebody to escalate to.
  • A small Node-specialist consultancy — higher rate, but they carry accountability for the outcome rather than the hours.
  • Someone from your own network, moonlighting — cheapest good option by a wide margin, if you have the person.
  • Upwork, top-rated-plus only — possible, but you do all the screening yourself.

Screen them in ten minutes

Three questions. The answers separate the people who can do this from the ones who will quietly break your ledger.

  1. “The store is a recursive Proxy over a plain object. Every route mutates it synchronously and commit() flushes before responding. How do you preserve that atomicity across two containers?”

    Good answer names row locks or serializable transactions and immediately asks about the read-modify-write window. “Just add Postgres” means they did not hear the question.

  2. “How will you prove you didn't break anything?”

    The only acceptable answer is “I run your 879 tests and they pass unmodified.” If they propose editing tests to fit the new store, end the call.

  3. “What happens to the deep-mutation catching in reactive()?”

    If they have not noticed it is a Proxy, they have not read the code they are quoting on.

How to buy it

Pilot one app, fixed price, before committing to the rest. Do vaco-notify first — no money moves through it, so a mistake is cheap, and it proves the adapter end to end. That is $1,500–3,000. Then, and only then, V3 and the money apps with real scrutiny.

Full job on the JSONB route: 80–150 hours, $8,000–26,000.

The frugal optionConsider not hiring anyone yet

You have 34 apps, not one app that needs 34 replicas. That is already horizontal. You scale by putting apps on more boxes and moving them around — not by running two copies of V3.

The one-container limit only bites when a single app outgrows a single container, and nothing here is close. Spend the $8–26k when a specific app is measurably straining. Until then it buys you nothing you can feel.
